Notes language:

1️⃣ Bond kyun banta hai? + Octet Rule

Bond tab stable ho sakta hai jab bonded arrangement ki energy separated particles se kam ho. Har isolated atom unstable nahin hota; atoms ko kuch "chahiye" nahin hota. Octet ek useful counting rule hai, universal cause nahin:

  • Octet rule (Kossel-Lewis): Kai main-group Lewis structures mein central atom ke around 8 valence electrons count hote hain, electron transfer ya sharing ke model mein. H ke liye duet; incomplete, odd-electron aur expanded-octet exceptions bhi hain.
  • Lewis symbols: element ke around dots = valence electrons. Na• + •Cl: → Na⁺ [Cl:]⁻ — Na ne 1 de diya, dono khush.
  • Electrovalency = kitne electrons transfer kiye (Na = 1, Mg = 2). Covalency = kitne share kiye (O = 2, N = 3).

⚠️ Octet ke exceptions (exam favourite):

  • Incomplete octet: BeCl₂ (4 e⁻), BF₃ (6 e⁻), LiCl (2 e⁻).
  • Expanded octet: Lewis counting mein PCl₅ ke P ke around 10 e⁻ aur SF₆ ke S ke around 12 e⁻. Yeh expanded-octet description hai, substantial d-orbital participation ka proof nahin.
  • Odd electrons: NO, NO₂, ClO₂ — inko free radicals kehte hain.
  • Noble gases bhi bond banate hain: XeF₂, XeOF₄ (octet rule chhichhora nikla 😄).

2️⃣ Ionic Bond — ek de, ek le

Metal (IE chhota, electron dena aasaan) + Non-metal (electron pakadne ka shauk) → transfer → ions → attraction → ionic bond. Jaise NaCl, MgO, CaF₂.

  • Lattice enthalpy: gaseous ions se 1 mol solid crystal banne par release energy. Jitni badi, utna strong crystal. Chhote ions + bade charge = zyada lattice enthalpy (MgO > NaCl).
  • Ionic solids ki properties: hard par brittle; melting point high; solid me conduct nahi, par pigla ya paani me ghula → conduct (ions free!); paani me soluble.

💡 Feel: Ionic bond = gudda-guddi ka gift exchange — ek ne de diya, doosre ne le liya, ab dono ek-doosre se chipke (opposite charges).

3️⃣ Covalent Bond — sharing is caring

Do non-metals electrons share karte hain: H₂ (1 pair = single), O₂ (2 pairs = double), N₂ (3 pairs = triple — bahut strong!).

  • Coordinate (dative) bond: dono electrons ek hi atom deta hai → arrow se dikhate hain: NH₃ → H⁺ = NH₄⁺, CO (C≡O me 1 bond coordinate).
  • Formal charge = valence e⁻ − [non-bonding e⁻ + ½ bonding e⁻]. Best Lewis structure = formal charges sabse chhoti (~0).
  • Resonance: jab ek structure kam pad jaaye — O₃, CO₃²⁻, benzene. Asli structure = sabka average (resonance hybrid) → zyada stable.
Bond order (approx) = total shared pairs / number of positions (resonance me)

🎯 O₃ me dono O-O bonds barabar hain (1.5 bond order) — resonance ka proof!

4️⃣ VSEPR — molecule ki SHAPE ⭐

Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion: central atom ke electron pairs ek-doosre ko dhakka dete hain → jitna door ho sakein, utna door → isi se shape banti hai.

Repulsion order: lone pair-lone pair > lone pair-bond pair > bond pair-bond pair
Bond pairsLone pairsShapeExample
20Linear (180°)CO₂, BeCl₂
30Trigonal planar (120°)BF₃
40Tetrahedral (109.5°)CH₄
31Pyramidal (~107°)NH₃
22Bent/V-shape (~104.5°)H₂O
50Trigonal bipyramidalPCl₅
60OctahedralSF₆

⚠️ Angle ka sawaal famous hai: CH₄ (109.5°) > NH₃ (107°) > H₂O (104.5°) — kyunki lone pairs zyada jagah ghere hote hain, angles daba dete hain!

5️⃣ Polarity — molecule biased hai ya nahi

  • Electronegativity ka difference → bond polar (shared electrons non-metal ki taraf). Difference bada (~1.7+) → ionic.
  • Dipole moment (μ): bond polarity ka vector sum. Symmetric molecules me cancel → non-polar: CO₂ (linear), BF₃, CCl₄.
  • Asymmetric → polar: H₂O (bent), NH₃ (pyramidal).

🎯 Trap: CO₂ ke bonds polar hain par molecule non-polar (vectors cancel). H₂O me cancel nahi hote — isliye paani polar = duniya ka best solvent!

6️⃣ VBT + Hybridization — orbitals ki mixing

Valence Bond Theory: bond tab banta hai jab orbitals overlap karte hain.

  • Sigma (σ) bond: head-on overlap (seedha) — strong, har single bond σ hota hai.
  • Pi (π) bond: side-by-side overlap — weak. Double bond = 1σ + 1π, triple = 1σ + 2π.

Hybridization: atom apne orbitals mix karke naye equal "hybrid" orbitals banata hai:

HybridShapeExample
spLinearBeCl₂, CO₂, C₂H₂
sp²Trigonal planarBF₃, C₂H₄
sp³TetrahedralCH₄, NH₃, H₂O
sp³dTrigonal bipyramidalPCl₅
sp³d²OctahedralSF₆

💡 Trick: VSEPR wali count (bond pairs + lone pairs) hi hybridization bata degi — 2=sp, 3=sp², 4=sp³, 5=sp³d, 6=sp³d². NH₃ aur H₂O dono sp³ hain (lone pairs bhi count!). Model note: PCl₅ = sp³d aur SF₆ = sp³d² conventional textbook/exam geometry labels hain; substantial d-orbital participation ka proof nahin.

7️⃣ MOT — Molecular Orbital Theory ⭐

VBT se aage: molecule banne par atomic orbitals milke molecular orbitals banate hain — electrons ab atom ke nahi, poore molecule ke!

  • Bonding MO (σ, π): low energy, stable. Antibonding MO (σ*, π*): high energy, todne wala.
  • Bond order = (bonding e⁻ − antibonding e⁻) / 2. Comparable species mein higher bond order aam taur par stronger, shorter bond se related hai. Simple MO covalent model mein BO > 0 net covalent bonding aur BO = 0 no net covalent bond batata hai; yeh universal existence/stability test nahin. He₂ ka bahut weak van der Waals dimer is simple picture ke bahar hai.
  • Order (N tak, Z≤7): σ1s σ*1s σ2s σ*2s π2p = π2p σ2p (π pehle!) • (O ke baad): … σ2p π2p = π2p (σ pehle). Ye change exam me pakka aata hai!
Bond order: N₂ = 3, O₂ = 2, F₂ = 1; He₂ = 0 (simple MO model: no net covalent bond)

⚡ O₂ ka paramagnetic hona — 2 unpaired electrons π* me → magnet ki taraf kheencha jaata hai. VBT isko explain nahi kar paayi, MOT ne kar diya — isliye MOT king hai. 👑

N₂(g) → 2N(g): bond dissociation enthalpy 946 kJ mol⁻¹; O₂(g) → 2O(g): 498 kJ mol⁻¹. Yeh gas-phase bond-cleavage energy costs hain, reaction rate ya activation barrier nahin. N₂ ki kinetic inertness ko sirf bond order ya in values se decide nahin kar sakte. Catalyst pathway/activation barrier badal sakta hai, in dissociation enthalpies ko nahin.

8️⃣ Hydrogen Bonding — kamzor par gamechanger

Usual strong examples mein H kisi F, O ya N se covalently bonded hota hai. Us H ka partial positive δ+ charge doosre molecule ke, ya usi molecule ke, electron-rich acceptor/lone pair se attraction kar sakta hai: hydrogen bonding. Yeh poora H⁺ nahin. Strength system par depend karti hai; fixed 10x ratio ya universal dipole-force ranking nahin.

  • Intermolecular: molecules ke beech — H₂O, HF, NH₃. Isliye paani 100°C par boil hota hai (warna gas hota room temp par!), barf paani par tairti hai (density kam!).
  • Intramolecular: same molecule ke andar — o-nitrophenol (isliye woh p-nitrophenol se kam boil karta hai).
  • DNA ki double helix bhi H-bonds par tiki hai — life hi H-bonding ki den hai. 🧬

F-O-N usual strong hydrogen-bonding examples ka useful shortcut hai, exhaustive rule nahin. Is foundation scope mein pure HCl ko H₂O/HF jaisi usual strong intermolecular hydrogen bonding ka example mat mano.

1️⃣ Bond क्यों बनता है? + अष्टक नियम (Octet Rule)

बंधित व्यवस्था की ऊर्जा अलग कणों से कम होने पर वह अधिक स्थिर हो सकती है। हर अलग परमाणु अस्थिर नहीं होता और परमाणुओं की कोई "इच्छा" नहीं होती। अष्टक उपयोगी गणना-नियम है, सार्वभौम कारण नहीं:

  • अष्टक नियम (Kossel-Lewis): कई मुख्य-समूह Lewis संरचनाओं में परमाणु के चारों ओर 8 संयोजक इलेक्ट्रॉन गिने जाते हैं, इलेक्ट्रॉन स्थानांतरण या साझेदारी के मॉडल में। H के लिए द्विक तथा अधूरे, विषम-इलेक्ट्रॉन और विस्तारित अष्टक के अपवाद हैं।
  • Lewis symbols: तत्व के around dots = valence इलेक्ट्रॉन। Na• + •Cl: → Na⁺ [Cl:]⁻ — Na ने 1 दे दिया, दोनों खुश।
  • Electrovalency = कितने इलेक्ट्रॉन transfer किए (Na = 1, Mg = 2)। Covalency = कितने share किए (O = 2, N = 3)।

⚠️ अष्टक के exceptions (exam favourite):

  • अधूरा अष्टक: BeCl₂ (4 e⁻), BF₃ (6 e⁻), LiCl (2 e⁻)।
  • विस्तारित अष्टक: Lewis गणना में PCl₅ के P के चारों ओर 10 e⁻ तथा SF₆ के S के चारों ओर 12 e⁻ हैं। यह विस्तारित अष्टक का वर्णन है, पर्याप्त d-कक्षक भागीदारी का प्रमाण नहीं।
  • Odd इलेक्ट्रॉन: NO, NO₂, ClO₂ — इन्हें free radicals कहते हैं।
  • Noble gases भी bond बनाती हैं: XeF₂, XeOF₄ (अष्टक नियम धरा रह गया 😄)।

2️⃣ आयनिक बंध (Ionic Bond) — एक दे, एक ले

Metal (IE छोटा, इलेक्ट्रॉन देना आसान) + Non-metal (इलेक्ट्रॉन पकड़ने का शौक) → transfer → ions → attraction → ionic bond। जैसे NaCl, MgO, CaF₂।

  • Lattice enthalpy: gaseous ions से 1 mol solid crystal बनने पर release energy। जितनी बड़ी, उतना strong crystal। छोटे ions + बड़े charge = ज़्यादा lattice enthalpy (MgO > NaCl)।
  • Ionic solids के गुण: hard पर brittle; melting point high; solid में conduct नहीं, पर पिघला या पानी में घुला → conduct (ions free!); पानी में soluble।

💡 Feel: Ionic bond = gift exchange — एक ने दे दिया, दूसरे ने ले लिया, अब दोनों एक-दूसरे से चिपके (opposite charges)।

3️⃣ सहसंयोजक बंध (Covalent Bond) — sharing is caring

दो non-metals इलेक्ट्रॉन share करते हैं: H₂ (1 pair = single), O₂ (2 pairs = double), N₂ (3 pairs = triple — बहुत strong!)।

  • उपसहसंयोजक (Coordinate/dative) bond: दोनों इलेक्ट्रॉन एक ही परमाणु देता है → arrow से दिखाते हैं: NH₃ → H⁺ = NH₄⁺, CO।
  • औपचारिक आवेश (Formal charge) = valence e⁻ − [non-bonding e⁻ + ½ bonding e⁻]। Best Lewis structure = formal charges सबसे छोटी (~0)।
  • अनुनाद (Resonance): जब एक structure कम पड़ जाए — O₃, CO₃²⁻, benzene। असली structure = सबका average (resonance hybrid) → ज़्यादा stable।
Bond order (approx) = कुल shared pairs / positions की संख्या (resonance में)

🎯 O₃ में दोनों O-O bonds बराबर हैं (1.5 bond order) — resonance का proof!

4️⃣ VSEPR — अणु की SHAPE ⭐

Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion: central परमाणु के इलेक्ट्रॉन pairs एक-दूसरे को धक्का देते हैं → जितना दूर हो सकें, उतना दूर → इसी से shape बनती है।

Repulsion order: lone pair-lone pair > lone pair-bond pair > bond pair-bond pair
Bond pairsLone pairsShapeउदाहरण
20Linear (180°)CO₂, BeCl₂
30Trigonal planar (120°)BF₃
40Tetrahedral (109.5°)CH₄
31Pyramidal (~107°)NH₃
22Bent/V-shape (~104.5°)H₂O
50Trigonal bipyramidalPCl₅
60OctahedralSF₆

⚠️ Angle का सवाल famous है: CH₄ (109.5°) > NH₃ (107°) > H₂O (104.5°) — क्योंकि lone pairs ज़्यादा जगह घेरते हैं, angles दबा देते हैं!

5️⃣ ध्रुवता (Polarity) — अणु biased है या नहीं

  • Electronegativity का difference → bond polar (shared इलेक्ट्रॉन non-metal की तरफ)। Difference बड़ा (~1.7+) → ionic।
  • द्विध्रुव आघूर्ण (Dipole moment, μ): bond polarity का vector sum। Symmetric अणुओं में cancel → non-polar: CO₂ (linear), BF₃, CCl₄।
  • Asymmetric → polar: H₂O (bent), NH₃ (pyramidal)।

🎯 Trap: CO₂ के bonds polar हैं पर अणु non-polar (vectors cancel)। H₂O में cancel नहीं होते — इसलिए पानी polar = दुनिया का best solvent!

6️⃣ VBT + संकरण (Hybridization) — orbitals की mixing

Valence Bond Theory: bond तब बनता है जब orbitals overlap करते हैं।

  • Sigma (σ) bond: head-on overlap (सीधा) — strong, हर single bond σ होता है।
  • Pi (π) bond: side-by-side overlap — weak। Double bond = 1σ + 1π, triple = 1σ + 2π।

संकरण (Hybridization): परमाणु अपने orbitals mix करके नए equal "hybrid" orbitals बनाता है:

HybridShapeउदाहरण
spLinearBeCl₂, CO₂, C₂H₂
sp²Trigonal planarBF₃, C₂H₄
sp³TetrahedralCH₄, NH₃, H₂O
sp³dTrigonal bipyramidalPCl₅
sp³d²OctahedralSF₆

💡 Trick: VSEPR वाली count (bond pairs + lone pairs) ही hybridization बता देगी — 2=sp, 3=sp², 4=sp³, 5=sp³d, 6=sp³d²। NH₃ और H₂O दोनों sp³ हैं (lone pairs भी count!)। मॉडल टिप्पणी: PCl₅ = sp³d और SF₆ = sp³d² पाठ्यपुस्तक/परीक्षा के पारंपरिक ज्यामिति-लेबल हैं; पर्याप्त d-कक्षक भागीदारी का प्रमाण नहीं।

7️⃣ MOT — आणविक कक्षक सिद्धांत ⭐

VBT से आगे: अणु बनने पर atomic orbitals मिलकर molecular orbitals बनाते हैं — इलेक्ट्रॉन अब परमाणु के नहीं, पूरे अणु के!

  • Bonding MO (σ, π): low energy, stable। Antibonding MO (σ*, π*): high energy, तोड़ने वाला।
  • बंध कोटि (Bond order) = (bonding e⁻ − antibonding e⁻) / 2। तुलनीय स्पीशीज़ में अधिक बंध-कोटि सामान्यतः अधिक मज़बूत, छोटे बंध से जुड़ी है। सरल MO सहसंयोजक मॉडल में BO > 0 शुद्ध सहसंयोजक आबंधन और BO = 0 उसका अभाव बताता है; यह अस्तित्व/स्थिरता की सार्वभौम कसौटी नहीं। He₂ का अत्यंत दुर्बल van der Waals द्वितय इस सरल चित्र के बाहर है।
  • Order (N तक, Z≤7): σ1s σ*1s σ2s σ*2s π2p = π2p σ2p (π पहले!) • (O के बाद): … σ2p π2p = π2p (σ पहले)। यह change exam में पक्का आता है!
Bond order: N₂ = 3, O₂ = 2, F₂ = 1; He₂ = 0 (सरल MO मॉडल: शुद्ध सहसंयोजक बंध नहीं)

⚡ O₂ का paramagnetic होना — 2 unpaired इलेक्ट्रॉन π* में → magnet की तरफ खिंचता है। VBT इसको explain नहीं कर पाई, MOT ने कर दिया — इसलिए MOT king है। 👑

N₂(g) → 2N(g) की बंध-वियोजन एन्थैल्पी 946 kJ mol⁻¹ तथा O₂(g) → 2O(g) की 498 kJ mol⁻¹ है। ये गैस-अवस्था में बंध तोड़ने की ऊर्जा-लागत हैं, अभिक्रिया-दर या सक्रियण अवरोध नहीं। N₂ की गतिज निष्क्रियता केवल बंध-क्रम या इन मानों से तय नहीं होती। उत्प्रेरक मार्ग/सक्रियण अवरोध बदल सकता है, इन वियोजन एन्थैल्पियों को नहीं।

8️⃣ हाइड्रोजन बंध (H-Bonding) — कमज़ोर पर gamechanger

सामान्य मज़बूत उदाहरणों में H, F, O या N से सहसंयोजक बंध द्वारा जुड़ा होता है। उसका आंशिक धन δ+ आवेश दूसरे अणु के, या उसी अणु के, इलेक्ट्रॉन-समृद्ध ग्राही/lone pair से आकर्षण कर सकता है: हाइड्रोजन आबंधन। H पूरा H⁺ नहीं है। शक्ति तंत्र पर निर्भर है; निश्चित 10x अनुपात या dipole बलों से सार्वभौम तुलना नहीं।

  • अंतराआण्विक (Intermolecular): अणुओं के बीच — H₂O, HF, NH₃। इसलिए पानी 100°C पर boil होता है (वर्ना room temp पर gas होता!), बर्फ पानी पर तैरती है (density कम!)।
  • अंतःआण्विक (Intramolecular): same अणु के अंदर — o-nitrophenol (इसलिए वह p-nitrophenol से कम boil करता है)।
  • DNA की double helix भी H-bonds पर टिकी है — जीवन ही H-bonding की देन है। 🧬

F-O-N सामान्य मज़बूत हाइड्रोजन आबंधन के उदाहरणों का उपयोगी संकेत है, संपूर्ण नियम नहीं। इस आधारभूत दायरे में शुद्ध HCl को H₂O/HF जैसे मज़बूत अंतराअणुक हाइड्रोजन आबंधन का उदाहरण न मानें।

1️⃣ Why do bonds form? + The Octet Rule

A bonded arrangement can be more stable when its energy is lower than that of the separated particles. Not every isolated atom is unstable, and atoms do not have wants. The octet is a useful counting rule, not a universal cause of bonding:

  • Octet rule (Kossel-Lewis): Many main-group Lewis structures count eight valence electrons around an atom through electron transfer or sharing. Hydrogen follows a duet; incomplete-octet, odd-electron and expanded-octet cases are exceptions.
  • Lewis symbols: dots around the element = valence electrons. Na• + •Cl: → Na⁺ [Cl:]⁻ — Na gave 1 away, both are happy.
  • Electrovalency = electrons transferred (Na = 1, Mg = 2). Covalency = electrons shared (O = 2, N = 3).

⚠️ Exceptions to the octet (exam favourite):

  • Incomplete octet: BeCl₂ (4 e⁻), BF₃ (6 e⁻), LiCl (2 e⁻).
  • Expanded octet: Lewis counting gives 10 e⁻ around P in PCl₅ and 12 e⁻ around S in SF₆. This expanded-octet description is not proof of substantial d-orbital participation.
  • Odd electrons: NO, NO₂, ClO₂ — these are free radicals.
  • Even noble gases bond: XeF₂, XeOF₄ (the octet rule takes a hit 😄).

2️⃣ Ionic Bond — one gives, one takes

A metal (low IE, gives electrons easily) + a non-metal (loves grabbing electrons) → transfer → ions → attraction → ionic bond. E.g. NaCl, MgO, CaF₂.

  • Lattice enthalpy: energy released when gaseous ions form 1 mol of solid crystal. The bigger it is, the stronger the crystal. Small ions + large charges = high lattice enthalpy (MgO > NaCl).
  • Properties of ionic solids: hard but brittle; high melting point; don't conduct when solid, but conduct when melted or dissolved (ions are free!); soluble in water.

💡 Feel: an ionic bond is a gift exchange — one gave, one took, and now the two stick together (opposite charges).

3️⃣ Covalent Bond — sharing is caring

Two non-metals share electrons: H₂ (1 pair = single), O₂ (2 pairs = double), N₂ (3 pairs = triple — very strong!).

  • Coordinate (dative) bond: both shared electrons come from one atom → shown with an arrow: NH₃ → H⁺ = NH₄⁺, CO.
  • Formal charge = valence e⁻ − [non-bonding e⁻ + ½ bonding e⁻]. The best Lewis structure has the smallest formal charges (~0).
  • Resonance: when one structure isn't enough — O₃, CO₃²⁻, benzene. The real structure is the average of all (resonance hybrid) → more stable.
Bond order (approx) = total shared pairs / number of positions (with resonance)

🎯 In O₃ both O-O bonds are equal (bond order 1.5) — proof of resonance!

4️⃣ VSEPR — the SHAPE of molecules ⭐

Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion: electron pairs around the central atom push each other as far apart as possible → that geometry decides the shape.

Repulsion order: lone pair-lone pair > lone pair-bond pair > bond pair-bond pair

⚠️ The angle question is famous: CH₄ (109.5°) > NH₃ (107°) > H₂O (104.5°) — lone pairs hog more space and squeeze the angles!

5️⃣ Polarity — is the molecule biased?

  • A difference in electronegativity → a polar bond (shared electrons lean towards the non-metal). A big difference (~1.7+) → ionic.
  • Dipole moment (μ): the vector sum of bond polarities. Symmetric molecules cancel out → non-polar: CO₂ (linear), BF₃, CCl₄.
  • Asymmetric → polar: H₂O (bent), NH₃ (pyramidal).

🎯 Trap: CO₂ has polar bonds but a non-polar molecule (the vectors cancel). In H₂O they don't cancel — that's why water is polar and the world's best solvent!

6️⃣ VBT + Hybridization — orbital mixing

Valence Bond Theory: a bond forms when orbitals overlap.

  • Sigma (σ) bond: head-on overlap — strong; every single bond is σ.
  • Pi (π) bond: side-by-side overlap — weak. Double bond = 1σ + 1π, triple = 1σ + 2π.

Hybridization: the atom mixes its orbitals into new, equal "hybrid" orbitals:

HybridShapeExample
spLinearBeCl₂, CO₂, C₂H₂

💡 Trick: the VSEPR count (bond pairs + lone pairs) gives the hybridization — 2=sp, 3=sp², 4=sp³, 5=sp³d, 6=sp³d². NH₃ and H₂O are both sp³ (lone pairs count!). Model note (added explanation): PCl₅ = sp³d and SF₆ = sp³d² are conventional textbook/exam geometry labels, not proof of substantial d-orbital participation.

7️⃣ MOT — Molecular Orbital Theory ⭐

Beyond VBT: when a molecule forms, atomic orbitals merge into molecular orbitals — the electrons now belong to the whole molecule!

  • Bonding MO (σ, π): low energy, stabilizing. Antibonding MO (σ*, π*): high energy, destabilizing.
  • Bond order = (bonding e⁻ − antibonding e⁻) / 2. For comparable species, higher bond order generally correlates with a stronger, shorter bond. In this simple covalent MO model, BO > 0 means net covalent bonding and BO = 0 means no net covalent bond. This is not a universal existence/stability test: the very weak van der Waals helium dimer lies outside this simple picture.
  • Order (up to N, Z≤7): σ1s σ*1s σ2s σ*2s π2p = π2p σ2p (π first!) • (from O onwards): … σ2p π2p = π2p (σ first). This switch always comes up in exams!
Bond order: N₂ = 3, O₂ = 2, F₂ = 1; He₂ = 0 (simple MO model: no net covalent bond)

⚡ O₂ is paramagnetic — 2 unpaired electrons in π* → it is pulled towards a magnet. VBT couldn't explain this; MOT could — that's why MOT wears the crown. 👑

N₂(g) → 2N(g) has a bond dissociation enthalpy of 946 kJ mol⁻¹; O₂(g) → 2O(g), 498 kJ mol⁻¹. These are gas-phase bond-cleavage energy costs, not reaction rates or activation barriers. Bond order or these values alone do not determine kinetic inertness. A catalyst can change the pathway/activation barrier, not these dissociation enthalpies.

8️⃣ Hydrogen Bonding — weak but a gamechanger

In the usual strong examples, H is covalently bonded to F, O or N. Its partial positive δ+ charge can be attracted to an electron-rich acceptor/lone pair in another molecule or in the same molecule: hydrogen bonding. This H is not a full H⁺ ion. Strength depends on the system; there is no fixed 10x ratio or universal ranking above dipole forces.

  • Intermolecular: between molecules — H₂O, HF, NH₃. That's why water boils at 100°C (it would be a gas at room temperature otherwise!) and ice floats (lower density!).
  • Intramolecular: inside one molecule — o-nitrophenol (which is why it boils lower than p-nitrophenol).
  • DNA's double helix is held together by H-bonds — life itself runs on H-bonding. 🧬

F-O-N is a useful shortcut for the usual strong examples, not an exhaustive rule. In this foundation scope, pure HCl is not an example of the usual strong intermolecular hydrogen bonding seen in H₂O/HF.

JEE Practice — 10 Questions

Pehle khud solve karo, phir answer dekho.

Q1 NH₃ aur H₂O ki shape aur bond angle batao (VSEPR).
Answer dekho

NH₃: trigonal pyramidal, ~107° (1 lone pair, N sp³). H₂O: bent/V-shape, ~104.5° (2 lone pairs, O sp³). Lone pairs angle ghata dete hain!

Q2 CH₄, BF₃, BeCl₂ me central atom ka hybridization aur shape?
Answer dekho

CH₄: sp³, tetrahedral (109.5°) • BF₃: sp², trigonal planar (120°) • BeCl₂: sp, linear (180°)

Q3 MOT se O₂ ka bond order aur magnetic nature?
Answer dekho

O₂ ke total 16 e⁻: (σ1s)² (σ*1s)² (σ2s)² (σ*2s)² (σ2pz)² (π2px)² (π2py)² (π*2px)¹ (π*2py)¹. Aakhri do degenerate π* orbitals mein ek-ek electron parallel spins ke saath (↑, ↑); bonding 10, antibonding 6 → BO = (10−6)/2 = 2. π* me 2 unpaired e⁻ → paramagnetic (Lewis structure ye explain nahi kar sakta!)

Q4 Inme kaun paramagnetic hai: N₂, O₂, B₂, F₂?
Answer dekho

O₂ aur B₂ — dono ke π* (O₂) ya π (B₂) me unpaired electrons. N₂ aur F₂ diamagnetic.

Q5 CO₂ me polar C=O bonds hote hue bhi net dipole moment zero kyun? H₂O ka kyun nahi zero?
Answer dekho

CO₂ linear hai — dono bond dipoles equal-opposite cancel. H₂O bent hai — dipoles cancel nahi hote, net μ ≈ 1.85 D.

Q6 H₂O (18 g/mol) ka boiling point H₂S (34 g/mol) se ZYADA kyun hai?
Answer dekho

H₂O mein usual strong intermolecular hydrogen bonding (O—H···O) hoti hai, H₂S mein waisi nahin. Dono mein dispersion forces hain; H₂S polar hone se dipole-dipole forces bhi hain. In pure substances ki comparison mein H₂O ki hydrogen-bond network uske higher boiling point ka main reason hai. Room temperature aur lagbhag 1 atm par H₂O liquid, H₂S gas.

Q7 NaCl, MgO, NaF, MgCl₂ me sabse zyada lattice energy kiski?
Answer dekho

MgO — lattice energy ∝ (z⁺z⁻)/r. Mg²⁺ aur O²⁻ dono doubly charged (z⁺z⁻ = 4) + chhota size → sabse strong lattice.

Q8 HNO₃ me N ka formal charge? (structure: HO–N(=O)–O)
Answer dekho

FC = valence − (non-bonding + ½ bonding) = 5 − (0 + 4) = +1 on N, single-bonded O⁻ pe −1. (Formula yaad rakho: FC = V − N − B/2)

Q9 CH₃–CH=CH–C≡CH me sigma aur pi bonds kitne?
Answer dekho

Count: har single bond 1σ, double = 1σ+1π, triple = 1σ+2π. Total: 10 σ + 3 π (skeleton ke 4σ (C-C) + 6σ (C-H) = 10σ; 1π + 2π = 3π)

Q10 Fajans rules se: NaCl, MgCl₂, AlCl₃ me sabse zyada covalent kaun?
Answer dekho

AlCl₃ — Al³⁺ ka chhota size + high charge → Cl⁻ ko zyada polarize karta hai → zyada covalent character.
