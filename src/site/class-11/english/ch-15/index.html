Long Notes — Full Explanation

Concept pakka karne ke liye — aaram se padho

Notes language:

1️⃣ Writer aur Story Parichay ⭐⭐

  • Marga Minco ⭐⭐: NCERT text mein lekhika ka naam hai; nationality aur janm-varsh text mein nahi hain (teaching background). Book ki intro: ye ek poignant kahani hai — ek beti War ke baad Holland mein apni maa ka saaman dhoondhne jaati hai.
  • Narrator ⭐⭐: pehle vyakti mein (“I”) bolti beti, jo “Mrs S’s daughter” kehkar apna parichay deti hai. Uska Jewish hona text mein seedha nahi likha; Hanukkah candle-holder se ye supported reading hai.
  • Kendriya sawaal ⭐⭐⭐: kya War ke baad ghar ki cheezein, jinse yaadein judi hain, wapas milne par bhi apna mulya rakhti hain? Narrator ka jawab ant mein: “instantly lose their value” (text).
  • Address ⭐⭐⭐: maa ne kaha tha: “In Marconi Street. Number 46. Remember that.” Darwaze par name-plate par Dorling likha hai (black letters, white enamel) aur jamb par Number 46.

💡 Marga Minco + narrator (“Mrs S’s daughter”) + Mrs Dorling + 46 Marconi Street + War ke baad ka Holland. Frame pakka.

2️⃣ Story Summary ⭐⭐⭐

  • Pehli visit ⭐⭐⭐: ek aurat darwaza “a chink” khol kar narrator ko dekhti rehti hai; “No, I don’t know you.” Wo maa ka green knitted cardigan pehne hai. Phir: “Have you come back? I thought that no one had come back.”
  • Darwaza band ⭐⭐⭐: “I regret I cannot do anything for you… It is not convenient for me now… Another time.” Wo darwaza dhire se band kar deti hai; andar se musty smell aati hai aur bay window ka curtain hilta hai.
  • Maa ki baat (flashback) ⭐⭐⭐: War ke pehle half mein narrator kuch din ghar aayi to kuch cheezein gayab dikhi. Maa ne Mrs Dorling ke baare mein bataya — purani jaan-pehchan, jo achanak aakar rishta phir se jodti hai aur regularly aati hai. Kya le gayi ⭐⭐⭐: maa ke shabdon mein: table silver ek baar mein, phir antique plates, large vases, aur crockery. Maa kehti hai ki Dorling ne khud suggest kiya: “If we have to leave here we shall lose everything.” Narrator poori tarah convinced nahi thi.
  • Liberation ke baad ⭐⭐: narrator ko pehle us “stored stuff” mein dilchaspi nahi thi aur wo darti thi — un cheezon se jo ek aisi connection ki thi jo ab nahi rahi. Dheere-dheere sab normal hua aur use un cheezon ko dekhne, chhune, yaad karne ki curiosity hui.
  • Doosri visit ⭐⭐⭐: about fifteen saal ki ladki darwaza kholti hai; narrator uske peeche jaati hai. Iron Hanukkah candle-holder mirror ke paas tangi hai (“We never used it”). Living room: “a room I knew and did not know”.
  • Room aur cheezein ⭐⭐⭐: narrator horrified hai; cheezein dekhna chahti thi par wo use oppress karti hain — tasteless arrangement, ugly furniture, muggy smell. Wo woollen table-cloth par burn mark dhoondh leti hai; white pot (gold border on lid), spoons ka box aur apple on the pewter plate wali still life pehchanti hai.
  • Ant ⭐⭐⭐: ladki kehti hai ye cheezein “antique” hain (“my mother says”). Narrator spoons ke baare mein poochti hai; ladki drawer kholne jaati hai. Narrator: “I was forgetting the time. I must catch my train.” Jaate hue wo jingling of spoons and forks sunti hai.

🎯 Sequence: pehli visit (chink, cardigan) → inkaar → maa ka flashback → War ke baad ka dar → doosri visit → living room → spoons → train → “I resolved to forget the address.”

3️⃣ Characters ⭐⭐⭐

  • Narrator ⭐⭐⭐: pehle vyakti mein sunati hai; darti hai, phir curious hoti hai; ghar ke andar horrified; ant mein address bhoolne ka nirnay. Uski soch ka sabse bada kaaran text mein: cheezein “severed” hone par value kho deti hain.
  • Mrs Dorling ⭐⭐: pehli visit mein pehchanne se inkaar, chup rehkar dekhna, mother’s cardigan pehne; darwaza band. Uske iraade ke baare mein text seedha kuch nahi kehta — iska padhna supported reading hai.
  • Dorling ki beti ⭐⭐: about fifteen; chai banati hai; cheezon ko “antique” kehti hai aur kehti hai “it wasn’t anything special”. Wo in cheezon ka purana itihaas nahi jaanti — ye reading hai, text mein nahi likha.
  • Narrator ki maa ⭐⭐: Dorling par bharosa karti hai: “It would simply be an insult to talk like that.” Narrator ke unconvinced hone par use reprovingly dekhti hai. Maa ke baad ke haal ke baare mein text kuch nahi kehta.

💡 Narrator = dar + curiosity + nirnay | Dorling = inkaar | beti = “antique” | maa = bharosa.

4️⃣ Themes ⭐⭐⭐

  • Yaadein aur cheezein ⭐⭐⭐: narrator: cheezein jo purani zindagi se judi hain, strange surroundings mein dekhne par “instantly lose their value” (text).
  • War ke baad ki zindagi ⭐⭐⭐: narrator ka small rented room, khidkiyon par black-out paper ke shreds, aur handful of cutlery — ye text ke ant ke details hain jo uske nirnay ko samjhate hain. Book ka Q4: human predicament that follows war.
  • Aage badhna (supported reading) ⭐⭐: narrator address bhoolne ka nirnay leti hai (“that would be the easiest” of all things to forget). Isse aage badhne ke taur par padha ja sakta hai.
  • Dorling ka rakhna (supported reading) ⭐⭐: maa ne bataya ki Dorling ne cheezein save karne ki baat khud kahi. Baad mein cheezein uske ghar mein dikhti hain — iske peeche iraada kya tha, text seedha nahi kehta; ise reading ki tarah likho.

🎯 Theme = yaad + nuksaan + nirnay. Conclusion mein “I resolved to forget the address” aur “severed… lose their value” quote karo. Dorling ke irade par pakka faisla mat likho.

1️⃣ लेखिका और कहानी ⭐⭐

  • मार्गा मिन्को ⭐⭐: NCERT text में लेखिका का नाम है; राष्ट्रीयता और जन्म-वर्ष text में नहीं हैं।
  • नैरेटर ⭐⭐: पहले पुरुष में बोलती बेटी, जो “Mrs S’s daughter” कहकर अपना परिचय देती है; यहूदी होना Hanukkah candle-holder से supported reading है।
  • पता ⭐⭐⭐: माँ: “In Marconi Street. Number 46. Remember that.” name-plate पर Dorling।

2️⃣ कहानी-सारांश ⭐⭐⭐

  • पहली भेंट ⭐⭐⭐: एक औरत दरवाज़ा “a chink” खोलकर कहती है “No, I don’t know you.”; वह माँ का green cardigan पहने है; “I thought that no one had come back.”
  • माँ की बात ⭐⭐⭐: Dorling ने table silver, antique plates, large vases, crockery रख लिए; माँ के अनुसार उसने खुद सुझाया था।
  • दूसरी भेंट ⭐⭐⭐: लगभग पंद्रह साल की लड़की अंदर ले जाती है; Hanukkah candle-holder, woollen table-cloth पर burn mark, spoons का box।
  • अंत ⭐⭐⭐: “I must catch my train.” जाते हुए चम्मचों की jingling सुनाई देती है; नैरेटर address भूलने का निर्णय लेती है।

3️⃣ पात्र ⭐⭐⭐

  • नैरेटर ⭐⭐⭐: पहले डरती है, फिर curious; कमरे में horrified; अंत में address भूलने का निर्णय।
  • मिसेज़ डोर्लिंग ⭐⭐: पहचानने से इनकार; उसके इरादे के बारे में text सीधे कुछ नहीं कहता (reading)।
  • बेटी ⭐⭐: लगभग पंद्रह साल की; चीज़ों को “antique” कहती है।

4️⃣ विषय ⭐⭐⭐

  • स्मृति ⭐⭐⭐: चीज़ें अलग माहौल में “instantly lose their value” (text)।
  • युद्ध के बाद ⭐⭐⭐: छोटा किराए का कमरा, black-out paper, मुट्ठी भर cutlery — book का Q4।
  • आगे बढ़ना (supported reading) ⭐⭐: नैरेटर address भूलने का निर्णय लेती है।

1. Writer and Story

  • Marga Minco ⭐⭐: the NCERT text gives the writer's name; nationality and birth year are not in it (teaching background). The book's note calls it a poignant account of a daughter's search for her mother's belongings in Holland after the War.
  • Narrator ⭐⭐: a first-person narrator, “Mrs S’s daughter”. Her being Jewish is a supported reading from the Hanukkah candle-holder.
  • Address ⭐⭐⭐: “In Marconi Street. Number 46. Remember that.” The name-plate says Dorling.

2. Story Summary

  • First visit ⭐⭐⭐: the woman opens the door “a chink”, says “No, I don’t know you.” and wears the narrator's mother's green cardigan.
  • Mother's account ⭐⭐⭐: Mrs Dorling took the table silver, antique plates, large vases and crockery; the mother says Mrs Dorling suggested it herself.
  • Second visit ⭐⭐⭐: a girl of about fifteen lets her in; the Hanukkah candle-holder, the table-cloth with a burn mark, a box of spoons.
  • End ⭐⭐⭐: “I must catch my train.” She hears the jingling of spoons and forks and resolves to forget the address.

3. Characters

  • Narrator ⭐⭐⭐: first afraid, then curious; horrified in the living room; resolves to forget the address.
  • Mrs Dorling ⭐⭐: refuses to recognise her; her motives are not stated in the text (reading).
  • Dorling's daughter ⭐⭐: about fifteen; calls the things “antique”.

4. Themes

  • Memory ⭐⭐⭐: objects “instantly lose their value” in strange surroundings (text).
  • After the War ⭐⭐⭐: a small rented room, black-out paper, a handful of cutlery - the book's Q4.
  • Moving on (supported reading) ⭐⭐: she resolves to forget the address.

Board Practice — 10 Questions

Pehle khud solve karo, phir answer dekho.

Q1 Who is the writer of The Address?
Answer dekho

Marga Minco — the NCERT text gives the name; nationality and dates are teaching background.

Q2 Where does the narrator go and why?
Answer dekho

To Mrs Dorling’s house, Number 46 Marconi Street, because she is curious about her mother's possessions kept at that address: “I wanted to see them, touch, remember.”

Q3 How does the woman react on the first visit?
Answer dekho

She opens the door “a chink”, says “No, I don’t know you.”, wears the mother's green cardigan, and says “I regret I cannot do anything for you.”

Q4 Who gave the narrator the address?
Answer dekho

Her mother: “In Marconi Street. Number 46. Remember that.”

Q5 What did Mrs Dorling take from the narrator's mother?
Answer dekho

The mother lists the table silver, antique plates, large vases and crockery; Mrs Dorling took something every time she left.

Q6 Why was the narrator afraid after the Liberation?
Answer dekho

She was afraid of being confronted with things that had belonged to a connection that no longer existed.

Q7 What does she notice in the house on the second visit?
Answer dekho

The Hanukkah candle-holder, the woollen table-cloth with a burn mark, the white pot, the box of spoons and the apple on the pewter plate.

Q8 How does the living room feel to her?
Answer dekho

“A room I knew and did not know.” She is horrified; the tasteless arrangement, ugly furniture and muggy smell oppress her.

Q9 Why does she resolve to forget the address?
Answer dekho

Objects “instantly lose their value when, severed from them, you see them again in strange surroundings”, and she has only a small rented room and a handful of cutlery.

Q10 What can one say about the story's theme?
Answer dekho

Book Q4 calls it a human predicament that follows war. Support your answer with quotes: the first-visit greeting, the mother's account, and the ending.
