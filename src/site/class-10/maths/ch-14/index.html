Six notes: theoretical probability, elementary and complementary events, impossible and sure events, cards and dice, length and area (starred examples), and the Note to the Reader.

1. Theoretical probability aur equally likely outcomes

Sikka ya die 'fair' ho to har outcome utna hi likely hota hai (equally likely). Har experiment mein outcomes equally likely nahin hote, jaise 4 red aur 1 blue ball wale bag mein red aur blue equally likely nahin, par is chapter mein hum maante hain ki outcomes equally likely hain. Class IX ki experimental probability trials par based thi. Theoretical (classical) probability: P(E) = (E ke favourable outcomes ki sankhya) / (experiment ke saare possible outcomes ki sankhya). Is definition ko book Pierre Simon Laplace (1795) ka batati hai.

Source basis: NCERT jemh114 Reprint 2026-27; our worked aid, not an official solution. Chhapa prishth: p202-203.

2. Elementary event aur complementary events

Sirf ek outcome wale event ko elementary event kehte hain. Ek experiment ke saare elementary events ki probabilities ka yog 1 hota hai. Kisi bhi event E ke liye 'not E' ko E̅ se likhte hain aur P(E) + P(E̅) = 1, yani P(E̅) = 1 − P(E). E aur E̅ complementary events hain. Jab 'kam se kam' wale event ki probability seedhe mushkil ho, to complement se nikalna aasan ho sakta hai (book ke Example 4, 7 aur 9 mein dikhaya gaya hai).

Source basis: NCERT jemh114 Reprint 2026-27; our worked aid, not an official solution. Chhapa prishth: p204-205.

3. Impossible event, sure event aur 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1

Jo event ho hi nahin sakta (jaise die par 8 aana) use impossible event kehte hain; uski probability 0 hoti hai. Jo event pakka hota hai (jaise die par 7 se chhota number aana) use sure ya certain event kehte hain; uski probability 1 hoti hai. Favourable outcomes ki sankhya kabhi possible outcomes ki sankhya se zyada nahin ho sakti, isliye 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1. Probability kabhi negative ya 1 se bada nahin hoti.

Source basis: NCERT jemh114 Reprint 2026-27; our worked aid, not an official solution. Chhapa prishth: p205-207.

4. Taash ke patte (52 cards) aur do dice

Taash ki deck mein 52 patte hote hain, 4 suits mein 13-13: spades, hearts, diamonds aur clubs. Clubs aur spades kaale hote hain, hearts aur diamonds laal. Har suit mein ace, king, queen, jack, 10, 9, 8, 7, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2 hote hain. King, queen aur jack face cards kehlate hain. Do dice (ek blue, ek grey) ek saath phenke jaayein to possible outcomes 6 × 6 = 36 hote hain, aur (1, 4) aur (4, 1) alag-alag outcomes hain.

Source basis: NCERT jemh114 Reprint 2026-27; our worked aid, not an official solution. Chhapa prishth: p207-208 and p209.

5. Jab outcomes ginne mein na aayein (Examples 10 aur 11, exam ke nazariye se nahin)

Kabhi outcome do numbers ke beech koi bhi number ya kisi region ka koi bhi point hota hai, jise ginna possible nahin. Tab book length ya area ka ratio leti hai: P(E) = (E ke favourable length ya area) / (kul length ya area). Example 10 mein music 2 minute mein kabhi bhi band ho sakta hai; pehle aadhe minute mein band hone ki probability (1/2) / 2 = 1/4. Example 11 mein helicopter ek rectangular region mein kahin gira; lake mein girne ki probability 7.5 / 40.5 = 5/27. Book in dono par '* Not from the examination point of view' likhti hai.

Source basis: NCERT jemh114 Reprint 2026-27; our worked aid, not an official solution. Chhapa prishth: p210-212.

6. Experimental aur theoretical probability (A Note to the Reader)

Experimental (empirical) probability is par based hai ki asal mein kya hua; theoretical probability kuchh assumptions ke aadhar par predict karti hai ki kya hoga. Trials badhne par dono ke lagbhag barabar hone ki ummeed hoti hai.

Source basis: NCERT jemh114 Reprint 2026-27; our worked aid, not an official solution. Chhapa prishth: p217.

1. सैद्धांतिक प्रायिकता और समप्रायिक परिणाम

सिक्का या पासा 'निष्पक्ष' हो तो हर परिणाम उतना ही संभावित होता है (समप्रायिक)। हर प्रयोग में परिणाम समप्रायिक नहीं होते, जैसे 4 लाल और 1 नीली गेंद वाले थैले में लाल और नीली समप्रायिक नहीं, पर इस अध्याय में हम मानते हैं कि परिणाम समप्रायिक हैं। कक्षा IX की प्रायोगिक प्रायिकता परीक्षणों पर आधारित थी। सैद्धांतिक (शास्त्रीय) प्रायिकता: P(E) = (E के अनुकूल परिणामों की संख्या) / (प्रयोग के सभी संभावित परिणामों की संख्या)। पुस्तक इस परिभाषा को पियरे साइमन लाप्लास (1795) की बताती है।

2. प्रारंभिक घटना और पूरक घटनाएँ

केवल एक परिणाम वाली घटना को प्रारंभिक घटना कहते हैं। एक प्रयोग की सभी प्रारंभिक घटनाओं की प्रायिकताओं का योग 1 होता है। किसी भी घटना E के लिए 'E नहीं' को E̅ से लिखते हैं और P(E) + P(E̅) = 1, यानी P(E̅) = 1 − P(E)। E और E̅ पूरक घटनाएँ हैं। जब 'कम से कम' वाली घटना की प्रायिकता सीधे निकालना कठिन हो, तो पूरक से निकालना आसान हो सकता है (पुस्तक के उदाहरण 4, 7 और 9 में दिखाया गया है)।

3. असंभव घटना, निश्चित घटना और 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1

जो घटना हो ही नहीं सकती (जैसे पासे पर 8 आना) उसे असंभव घटना कहते हैं; उसकी प्रायिकता 0 होती है। जो घटना निश्चित होती है (जैसे पासे पर 7 से छोटी संख्या आना) उसे निश्चित घटना कहते हैं; उसकी प्रायिकता 1 होती है। अनुकूल परिणामों की संख्या कभी संभावित परिणामों की संख्या से अधिक नहीं हो सकती, इसलिए 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1। प्रायिकता कभी ऋणात्मक या 1 से बड़ी नहीं होती।

1. Theoretical probability and equally likely outcomes

If a coin or die is 'fair', every outcome is as likely as the others (equally likely). Not every experiment has equally likely outcomes: in a bag with 4 red balls and 1 blue ball, red and blue are not equally likely. But in this chapter we assume the outcomes are equally likely. The Class IX experimental probability was based on trials. The theoretical (classical) probability is P(E) = (number of outcomes favourable to E) / (number of all possible outcomes of the experiment). The book attributes this definition to Pierre Simon Laplace (1795).

Source basis: NCERT jemh114 Reprint 2026-27; our worked aid, not an official solution. Printed page: p202-203.

2. Elementary events and complementary events

An event with only one outcome is an elementary event. The sum of the probabilities of all the elementary events of an experiment is 1. For any event E, 'not E' is written E-bar, and P(E) + P(not E) = 1, so P(not E) = 1 − P(E). E and not E are complementary events. When the probability of an 'at least' event is hard to get directly, the complement can be easier (the book does this in Examples 4, 7 and 9).

Source basis: NCERT jemh114 Reprint 2026-27; our worked aid, not an official solution. Printed page: p204-205.

3. Impossible event, sure event and 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1

An event that cannot happen (such as getting 8 on a die) is an impossible event; its probability is 0. An event that is sure to happen (such as getting a number less than 7 on a die) is a sure or certain event; its probability is 1. The number of favourable outcomes is never more than the number of all possible outcomes, so 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1. A probability is never negative and never more than 1.

Source basis: NCERT jemh114 Reprint 2026-27; our worked aid, not an official solution. Printed page: p205-207.

4. Playing cards (52 cards) and two dice

A deck has 52 cards in 4 suits of 13 each: spades, hearts, diamonds and clubs. Clubs and spades are black, hearts and diamonds are red. Each suit has ace, king, queen, jack, 10, 9, 8, 7, 6, 5, 4, 3 and 2. Kings, queens and jacks are called face cards. When two dice (one blue, one grey) are thrown together, there are 6 × 6 = 36 possible outcomes, and (1, 4) is a different outcome from (4, 1).

Source basis: NCERT jemh114 Reprint 2026-27; our worked aid, not an official solution. Printed page: p207-208 and p209.

5. When outcomes cannot be counted (Examples 10 and 11, not from the examination point of view)

Sometimes the outcome is any number between two numbers or any point in a region, and these cannot be counted. Then the book uses a ratio of length or area: P(E) = (length or area favourable to E) / (total length or area). In Example 10 the music can stop at any time within 2 minutes; the probability that it stops in the first half-minute is (1/2) / 2 = 1/4. In Example 11 a helicopter crashed somewhere in a rectangular region; the probability that it crashed in the lake is 7.5 / 40.5 = 5/27. The book marks both with '* Not from the examination point of view'.

Source basis: NCERT jemh114 Reprint 2026-27; our worked aid, not an official solution. Printed page: p210-212.

6. Experimental and theoretical probability (A Note to the Reader)

The experimental (empirical) probability is based on what has actually happened, while the theoretical probability tries to predict what will happen from certain assumptions. As the number of trials increases, the two are expected to be nearly the same.

Source basis: NCERT jemh114 Reprint 2026-27; our worked aid, not an official solution. Printed page: p217.

Example 1: sikke par head aur tail

Problem: Ek sikka ek baar uchhalte hain. Head aane ki probability nikalo. Tail aane ki probability bhi nikalo.

Pehle saare possible outcomes gino, phir favourable outcomes gino, phir P(E) = favourable / total likho.

Steps:

Total possible outcomes: Head (H), Tail (T) = 2 E = 'head aana': favourable outcomes = 1 P(E) = P(head) = 1/2 F = 'tail aana': favourable outcomes = 1 P(F) = P(tail) = 1/2

Answer: P(head) = 1/2, P(tail) = 1/2

Hamari check: 1/2 + 1/2 = 1, jaisa complementary events ke liye hona chahiye.

Source basis: NCERT jemh114 Reprint 2026-27; our worked aid, not an official solution. Chhapa prishth: p204.

Example 2: bag se ek ball

Problem: Ek bag mein ek red, ek blue aur ek yellow ball hai (sab same size). Kritika bina dekhe ek ball nikalti hai. Probability nikalo ki wo (i) yellow, (ii) red, (iii) blue ball nikaale.

Pehle saare possible outcomes gino, phir favourable outcomes gino, phir P(E) = favourable / total likho.

Steps:

Total possible outcomes: 1 + 1 + 1 = 3 (i) P(Y) = 1/3 (ii) P(R) = 1/3 (iii) P(B) = 1/3

Answer: (i) 1/3 (ii) 1/3 (iii) 1/3

Hamari check: 1/3 + 1/3 + 1/3 = 1 (teen elementary events ka yog).

Source basis: NCERT jemh114 Reprint 2026-27; our worked aid, not an official solution. Chhapa prishth: p204-205.

Example 3: die par 4 se bada aur 4 se chhota ya barabar

Problem: Ek die ek baar phenkte hain. (i) 4 se bada number aane ki probability? (ii) 4 se chhota ya barabar number aane ki probability?

Pehle saare possible outcomes gino, phir favourable outcomes gino, phir P(E) = favourable / total likho.

Steps:

Total possible outcomes: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 = 6 (i) E: 4 se bada. Favourable: 5, 6, yani 2 P(E) = 2/6 = 1/3 (ii) F: 4 se chhota ya barabar. Favourable: 1, 2, 3, 4, yani 4 P(F) = 4/6 = 2/3

Answer: (i) 1/3 (ii) 2/3

Hamari check: 1/3 + 2/3 = 1; F wahi hai jo 'not E' hai.

Source basis: NCERT jemh114 Reprint 2026-27; our worked aid, not an official solution. Chhapa prishth: p205.

Example 4: taash ka ace

Problem: 52 patton ki well-shuffled deck se ek patta khinchte hain. Probability nikalo ki patta (i) ace ho, (ii) ace na ho.

Pehle saare possible outcomes gino, phir favourable outcomes gino, phir P(E) = favourable / total likho.

Steps:

Total possible outcomes: 52 patte = 52 (i) Deck mein 4 ace hain, favourable = 4 P(E) = 4/52 = 1/13 (ii) Ace na hone wale patte = 52 − 4 = 48 P(F) = 48/52 = 12/13 Dusra tareeka: P(F) = 1 − P(E) P(F) = 1 − 1/13 = 12/13

Answer: (i) 1/13 (ii) 12/13

Source basis: NCERT jemh114 Reprint 2026-27; our worked aid, not an official solution. Chhapa prishth: p207-208.

Example 5: tennis match

Problem: Sangeeta aur Reshma tennis match khelti hain. Sangeeta ke jeetne ki probability 0.62 hai. Reshma ke jeetne ki probability kya hai?

Pehle dekho 'not E' (complement) aasan hai ya nahin, phir P(not E) = 1 − P(E) lagao.

Steps:

S = Sangeeta jeete, R = Reshma jeete; R aur S complementary events hain. P(R) = 1 − P(S) = 1 − 0.62 = 0.38

Answer: P(Reshma jeetegi) = 0.38

Source basis: NCERT jemh114 Reprint 2026-27; our worked aid, not an official solution. Chhapa prishth: p208.

Example 6: do doston ka birthday

Problem: Savita aur Hamida dost hain. Probability nikalo ki dono ka (i) alag birthday, (ii) same birthday ho (leap year ignore karo).

Pehle dekho 'not E' (complement) aasan hai ya nahin, phir P(not E) = 1 − P(E) lagao.

Steps:

Savita ka birthday koi bhi din ho sakta hai. Hamida ka birthday bhi 365 dinon mein se koi bhi; hum ye 365 outcomes equally likely maante hain. (i) Hamida ka birthday Savita se alag: favourable = 365 − 1 = 364 P(different) = 364/365 (ii) Same birthday = 'different' ka complement P(same) = 1 − 364/365 = 1/365

Answer: (i) 364/365 (ii) 1/365

Source basis: NCERT jemh114 Reprint 2026-27; our worked aid, not an official solution. Chhapa prishth: p208.

Example 7: class representative

Problem: Class X mein 40 students hain, 25 ladkiyan aur 15 ladke. Teacher har student ka naam alag card par likhkar bag mein daalti hai aur ek card nikalti hai. Probability nikalo ki card par (i) ladki, (ii) ladke ka naam ho.

Pehle saare possible outcomes gino, phir favourable outcomes gino, phir P(E) = favourable / total likho.

Steps:

Total possible outcomes: 40 cards = 40 (i) Ladki ke naam wale cards = 25 P(Girl) = 25/40 = 5/8 (ii) Ladke ke naam wale cards = 15 P(Boy) = 15/40 = 3/8 Dusra tareeka: P(Boy) = 1 − P(Girl) 1 − 5/8 = 3/8

Answer: (i) 5/8 (ii) 3/8

Source basis: NCERT jemh114 Reprint 2026-27; our worked aid, not an official solution. Chhapa prishth: p209.

Example 8: box mein marbles

Problem: Ek box mein 3 blue, 2 white aur 4 red marbles hain. Ek marble random nikalte hain. Probability nikalo ki wo (i) white, (ii) blue, (iii) red ho.

Pehle saare possible outcomes gino, phir favourable outcomes gino, phir P(E) = favourable / total likho.

Steps:

Total possible outcomes: 3 + 2 + 4 = 9 (i) P(W) = 2/9 (ii) P(B) = 3/9 = 1/3 (iii) P(R) = 4/9 P(W) + P(B) + P(R) = 2/9 + 3/9 + 4/9 = 1

Answer: (i) 2/9 (ii) 1/3 (iii) 4/9

Source basis: NCERT jemh114 Reprint 2026-27; our worked aid, not an official solution. Chhapa prishth: p209.

Example 9: do sikke, kam se kam ek head

Problem: Harpreet do alag sikke (₹ 1 aur ₹ 2) ek saath uchhalti hai. Kam se kam ek head aane ki probability kya hai?

Pehle saare possible outcomes gino, phir favourable outcomes gino, phir P(E) = favourable / total likho.

Steps:

Total possible outcomes: (H, H), (H, T), (T, H), (T, T) = 4 'Kam se kam ek head' ke favourable: (H, H), (H, T), (T, H), yani 3 P(E) = 3/4 Dusra tareeka: 'no head' sirf (T, T), P = 1/4 P(E) = 1 − 1/4 = 3/4

Answer: 3/4

Source basis: NCERT jemh114 Reprint 2026-27; our worked aid, not an official solution. Chhapa prishth: p210.

Example 10*: music kab ruka (length se)

Problem: Musical chair mein music rokne wale ko 2 minute ke andar kabhi bhi music rokna hai. Probability nikalo ki music shuru hone ke pehle aadhe minute mein ruke. (* Book ke anusar exam ke nazariye se nahin.)

Pehle saare possible outcomes gino, phir favourable outcomes gino, phir P(E) = favourable / total likho.

Steps:

Possible outcomes: 0 se 2 tak ke saare numbers (number line par lambai 2). Favourable: 0 se 1/2 tak, lambai 1/2. P(E) = (favourable length) / (total length) = (1/2) / 2 = 1/4

Answer: 1/4

Summary (printed 14.2): what this chapter covered

  1. The theoretical (classical) probability of an event E is P(E) = (number of outcomes favourable to E) / (number of all possible outcomes of the experiment), where the outcomes are assumed to be equally likely.
  2. The probability of a sure (certain) event is 1.
  3. The probability of an impossible event is 0.
  4. The probability of an event E is a number P(E) such that 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1.
  5. An event having only one outcome is called an elementary event. The sum of the probabilities of all the elementary events of an experiment is 1.
  6. For any event E, P(E) + P(not E) = 1, where not E is written E-bar. E and not E are called complementary events.
