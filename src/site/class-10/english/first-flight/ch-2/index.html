Nelson Mandela: Long Walk to Freedom

Mandela's inauguration in 1994, the end of apartheid, courage, love and freedom, and the poem A Tiger in the Zoo

Notes language:

1️⃣ Background aur Inauguration ⭐⭐⭐

  • Apartheid ek political system hai jo logon ko unki race ke aadhar par alag karta hai. Chapter ke anusar Mandela aur unki African National Congress ne apartheid ke khilaf poori zindagi ladai ki; Mandela ko tees saal jail mein rehna pada.
  • Chapter ke anusar 1994 mein South Africa mein pehle democratic elections hue aur Mandela pehle black President bane. Ye extract unki autobiography Long Walk to Freedom se hai.
  • BBC ki 10 May 1994 ki khabar (chapter mein di gayi): ANC ne 400 mein se 252 seats jeeti; inauguration Pretoria ke Union Buildings ke amphitheatre mein hua, 140 se zyada deshon ke dignitaries aaye.

💡 Apartheid + 1994 elections + Mandela pehle black President + Union Buildings, Pretoria.

2️⃣ Inauguration ka Din ⭐⭐⭐

  • 10 May ka din bright aur clear tha. Mandela ke saath unki beti Zenani thi. Union Buildings pehle seat of white supremacy the aur ab nayi democratic, non-racial government ki installation ka rainbow gathering ban gaye.
  • Pehle Mr de Klerk second deputy president bane, phir Thabo Mbeki first deputy president. Phir Mandela ne Constitution ko uphold karne ki pledge li.
  • Mandela ne kaha ki ye ek common victory for justice, peace aur human dignity hai, aur ki ab kabhi is beautiful land mein ek ka dusre par oppression nahi hoga. Ant: "Let freedom reign. God bless Africa!"
  • Jets aur helicopters ka display military ki democracy ke prati loyalty dikhata tha. Generals ne salute kiya, jabki kuch saal pehle wo Mandela ko arrest karte.

💡 Rainbow gathering, common victory, oppression never again, military ki loyalty.

3️⃣ Do Anthems aur Sense of History ⭐⭐⭐

  • Din ka symbol: do national anthems. Whites ne 'Nkosi Sikelel iAfrika' gaya aur blacks ne purana anthem 'Die Stem'. Dono groups ko lyrics nahi aati thi, par chapter ke anusar wo jald yaad kar lenge.
  • Mandela sense of history se overwhelmed the: twentieth century ke pehle dashak mein safed logon ne racial domination ka system banaya, jo duniya ke sabse harsh societies mein se ek tha. Aakhri dashak mein wo system overturn ho gaya.
  • Mandela ne kaha ki wo un sab African patriots ka sum hain jo unse pehle gaye; unka long and noble line unke saath dobara shuru hua.

💡 Do anthems = reconciliation ka symbol; racial domination ka ant.

4️⃣ Courage, Oppression aur Love ⭐⭐⭐

  • Apartheid ne ek deep and lasting wound diya, par oppression ke unintended effect se Oliver Tambo, Walter Sisulu, Chief Luthuli, Yusuf Dadoo, Bram Fischer, Robert Sobukwe jaise log paida hue. Mandela: "depths of oppression" se "heights of character".
  • Mandela ke anusar South Africa ki sabse badi wealth uske log hain, purest diamonds se bhi finer.
  • Courage: darr ka na hona nahi, balki darr par jeet. Brave wo hai jo darr ko conquer karta hai.
  • Love vs hate: koi paida hone se hi nafrat nahi karta; nafrat seekhni padti hai aur agar seekh sakte hain to pyaar bhi sikhaya ja sakta hai. Jail mein guards mein glimmer of humanity dikhi. "Man's goodness is a flame that can be hidden but never extinguished."

💡 Courage = fear par jeet; hate learned hai, love sikhaya ja sakta hai.

5️⃣ Freedom ka Matlab ⭐⭐⭐

  • Twin obligations: parivar ke prati aur apne logon, community, country ke prati. South Africa mein ek man of colour ke liye dono nibhana lagbhag impossible tha; wo twilight existence of secrecy and rebellion jeene par majboor tha.
  • Bachpan ki freedom: Mandela free paida hue (khet mein daudna, stream mein tairna), par chapter ke anusar wo illusion thi.
  • Student ke dino mein freedom sirf apne liye (transitory freedoms: raat bahar rehna, padhna, jaana). Johannesburg mein young man ke roop mein basic and honourable freedoms: potential achieve karna, kamana, shaadi aur parivar.
  • Phir unhone dekha ki unke saath sabki freedom curtailed hai, aur wo ANC mein shamil hue; ye hunger for freedom of my people ban gayi. Chapter ke anusar oppressor aur oppressed dono apni humanity khote hain.

💡 Twin obligations; transitory vs basic freedoms; oppressed aur oppressor dono unfree.

6️⃣ Poem: A Tiger in the Zoo ⭐⭐⭐

  • Poet Leslie Norris. Poem zoo ke tiger aur jungle ke tiger ko contrast karti hai, aur zoo se jungle aur wapas zoo tak jaati hai.
  • Zoo mein tiger apne vivid stripes ke saath cage ke kuch kadam chalta hai, velvet quiet pads par, apne quiet rage mein.
  • Jungle mein use shadow mein lurk karna chahiye, long grass mein sarakna chahiye, water hole ke paas plump deer ka intezaar karna chahiye, aur gaon ko terrorise karna chahiye.
  • Ant mein wo concrete cell mein band hai, visitors ko ignore karta hai, raat ko patrolling cars sunta hai aur apni brilliant eyes se brilliant stars ko dekhta hai. Chapter ke anusar 'quiet' aur 'brilliant' ka repetition poochha gaya hai.

💡 Zoo vs jungle contrast; quiet rage; brilliant eyes aur brilliant stars.

1️⃣ पृष्ठभूमि और शपथ-ग्रहण ⭐⭐⭐

  • रंगभेद (अपार्थाइड) ऐसी राजनीतिक व्यवस्था है जो लोगों को नस्ल के आधार पर अलग करती है। अध्याय के अनुसार मंडेला और उनकी अफ़्रीकन नेशनल कांग्रेस ने जीवन भर इसके ख़िलाफ़ संघर्ष किया; मंडेला को तीस साल जेल में रहना पड़ा।
  • अध्याय के अनुसार 1994 में दक्षिण अफ़्रीका में पहले लोकतांत्रिक चुनाव हुए और मंडेला पहले अश्वेत राष्ट्रपति बने। यह अंश उनकी आत्मकथा Long Walk to Freedom से है।
  • अध्याय में दी गई 10 मई 1994 की BBC ख़बर: ANC ने 400 में से 252 सीटें जीतीं; शपथ-ग्रहण प्रिटोरिया के यूनियन बिल्डिंग्स के एम्फ़ीथिएटर में हुआ, जिसमें 140 से अधिक देशों के प्रतिनिधि आए।

💡 रंगभेद, 1994 के चुनाव, मंडेला पहले अश्वेत राष्ट्रपति, प्रिटोरिया का यूनियन बिल्डिंग्स।

2️⃣ शपथ-ग्रहण का दिन ⭐⭐⭐

  • 10 मई का दिन उजला और साफ़ था। मंडेला के साथ उनकी बेटी ज़ेनानी थी। यूनियन बिल्डिंग्स पहले श्वेत वर्चस्व का केंद्र थे और अब पहली लोकतांत्रिक, ग़ैर-नस्लीय सरकार के लिए इंद्रधनुषी जमावड़े की जगह बन गए।
  • श्री डी क्लर्क दूसरे उप-राष्ट्रपति और थाबो म्बेकी पहले उप-राष्ट्रपति बने। फिर मंडेला ने संविधान का पालन करने की शपथ ली।
  • उन्होंने इसे न्याय, शांति और मानव गरिमा की साझा जीत कहा और कहा कि इस सुंदर धरती पर फिर कभी एक का दूसरे पर अत्याचार नहीं होगा। अंत: "Let freedom reign. God bless Africa!"
  • जेट और हेलीकॉप्टरों का प्रदर्शन सेना की लोकतंत्र के प्रति वफ़ादारी दिखाता था। जनरलों ने सलामी दी, जबकि कुछ साल पहले वे मंडेला को गिरफ़्तार करते।

💡 इंद्रधनुषी जमावड़ा, साझा जीत, अत्याचार फिर कभी नहीं, सेना की वफ़ादारी।

3️⃣ दो राष्ट्रगान और इतिहास का बोध ⭐⭐⭐

  • दिन का प्रतीक: दो राष्ट्रगान। गोरों ने 'Nkosi Sikelel iAfrika' गाया और अश्वेतों ने पुराना गान 'Die Stem'। दोनों समूहों को बोल नहीं आते थे, पर अध्याय के अनुसार वे जल्द रट लेंगे।
  • मंडेला इतिहास के बोध से अभिभूत थे: बीसवीं सदी के पहले दशक में श्वेत लोगों ने नस्लीय प्रभुत्व की व्यवस्था बनाई, जो दुनिया के सबसे कठोर समाजों में से एक थी। सदी के अंतिम दशक में वह पलट दी गई।
  • उन्होंने कहा कि वे उन सब अफ़्रीकी देशभक्तों का योग हैं जो उनसे पहले गए; उनकी लंबी और महान कड़ी उनके साथ फिर शुरू हुई।

💡 दो राष्ट्रगान = सुलह का प्रतीक; नस्लीय प्रभुत्व का अंत।

4️⃣ साहस, अत्याचार और प्रेम ⭐⭐⭐

  • रंगभेद ने गहरा और स्थायी घाव दिया, पर अत्याचार के अनचाहे असर से ओलिवर टैम्बो, वॉल्टर सिसुलु, चीफ़ लुतुली, यूसुफ़ दादू, ब्राम फ़िशर, रॉबर्ट सोबुकवे जैसे लोग पैदा हुए। मंडेला: "अत्याचार की गहराइयों" से "चरित्र की ऊँचाइयाँ"।
  • उनके अनुसार देश की सबसे बड़ी दौलत उसके लोग हैं, शुद्धतम हीरों से भी बेहतर।
  • साहस डर का न होना नहीं, बल्कि डर पर जीत है; बहादुर वह है जो डर को जीतता है।
  • प्रेम और नफ़रत: कोई नफ़रत लेकर पैदा नहीं होता; नफ़रत सीखनी पड़ती है, और अगर सीख सकते हैं तो प्रेम भी सिखाया जा सकता है। जेल में एक पहरेदार में उन्होंने इंसानियत की झलक देखी। "Man's goodness is a flame that can be hidden but never extinguished."

💡 साहस = डर पर जीत; नफ़रत सीखी जाती है, प्रेम सिखाया जा सकता है।

5️⃣ आज़ादी का मतलब ⭐⭐⭐

  • दोहरे दायित्व: परिवार के प्रति और अपने लोगों, समुदाय, देश के प्रति। दक्षिण अफ़्रीका में एक अश्वेत के लिए दोनों निभाना लगभग असंभव था; उसे गोपनीयता और विद्रोह के धुँधलके जीवन में जीना पड़ता था।
  • बचपन की आज़ादी: मंडेला आज़ाद पैदा हुए (खेतों में दौड़ना, धारा में तैरना), पर अध्याय के अनुसार वह एक भ्रम थी।
  • विद्यार्थी जीवन में आज़ादी केवल अपने लिए थी (क्षणिक आज़ादियाँ: रात बाहर रहना, पढ़ना, कहीं भी जाना)। जोहानिसबर्ग में युवा के रूप में बुनियादी और सम्मानजनक आज़ादियाँ: क्षमता पाना, कमाना, शादी और परिवार।
  • फिर उन्होंने देखा कि उनके भाई-बहन भी आज़ाद नहीं हैं, और वे ANC में शामिल हुए; अपनी आज़ादी की भूख अपने लोगों की आज़ादी की भूख बन गई। अध्याय के अनुसार उत्पीड़क और उत्पीड़ित दोनों अपनी मानवता खो देते हैं।

💡 दोहरे दायित्व; क्षणिक और बुनियादी आज़ादी; उत्पीड़क और उत्पीड़ित दोनों अनाज़ाद।

6️⃣ कविता: A Tiger in the Zoo ⭐⭐⭐

  • कवि लेस्ली नॉरिस। कविता चिड़ियाघर के बाघ और जंगल के बाघ की तुलना करती है और चिड़ियाघर से जंगल और फिर चिड़ियाघर तक जाती है।
  • चिड़ियाघर में बाघ अपनी चटकीली धारियों के साथ पिंजरे के कुछ क़दम चलता है, मख़मली चुप पंजों पर, अपने शांत क्रोध में।
  • जंगल में उसे छाया में दुबकना, लंबी घास में सरकना, पानी के पास मोटे हिरणों की ताक में रहना और गाँव को आतंकित करना चाहिए।
  • अंत में वह कंक्रीट की कोठरी में बंद है, आगंतुकों को अनदेखा करता है, रात में गश्ती गाड़ियाँ सुनता है और अपनी चमकती आँखों से चमकते तारों को देखता है। अध्याय 'quiet' और 'brilliant' के दोहराव का प्रभाव पूछता है।

💡 चिड़ियाघर बनाम जंगल की तुलना; शांत क्रोध; चमकती आँखें और चमकते तारे।

1️⃣ Background and the inauguration ⭐⭐⭐

  • Apartheid is a political system that separates people according to their race. The chapter says Mandela and his African National Congress spent a lifetime fighting it, and Mandela spent thirty years in prison.
  • The chapter says democratic elections were held in South Africa in 1994 and Mandela became the first black President. The extract is from his autobiography Long Walk to Freedom.
  • The BBC news item of 10 May 1994 given in the chapter: the ANC won 252 of the 400 seats; the inauguration was in the Union Buildings amphitheatre in Pretoria, attended by dignitaries from more than 140 countries.

💡 Apartheid, the 1994 elections, Mandela the first black President, the Union Buildings in Pretoria.

2️⃣ The day of the inauguration ⭐⭐⭐

  • 10 May dawned bright and clear and Mandela was accompanied by his daughter Zenani. The Union Buildings had been the seat of white supremacy and were now the site of a rainbow gathering for the installation of the first democratic, non-racial government.
  • Mr de Klerk was sworn in as second deputy president and Thabo Mbeki as first deputy president. Mandela then pledged to obey and uphold the Constitution.
  • He called the day a common victory for justice, for peace, for human dignity, and said that never again would this beautiful land experience the oppression of one by another. He ended: "Let freedom reign. God bless Africa!"
  • The fly-past of jets and helicopters was a demonstration of the military's loyalty to democracy. The generals saluted him, though not many years before they would have arrested him.

💡 Rainbow gathering, common victory, oppression never again, the military's loyalty.

3️⃣ Two anthems and a sense of history ⭐⭐⭐

  • The day was symbolised by two national anthems: whites singing 'Nkosi Sikelel iAfrika' and blacks singing 'Die Stem', the old anthem. Neither group knew the lyrics of the anthem it once despised, but the chapter says they would soon know them by heart.
  • Mandela was overwhelmed with a sense of history: in the first decade of the twentieth century the white-skinned peoples built a system of racial domination, one of the harshest societies the world has known. In the last decade of the century it was overturned.
  • He felt he was the sum of all those African patriots who had gone before him, and that their long and noble line began again with him.

💡 Two anthems as a sign of reconciliation; the end of racial domination.

4️⃣ Courage, oppression and love ⭐⭐⭐

  • Apartheid left a deep and lasting wound, but its unintended effect was to produce leaders such as Oliver Tambo, Walter Sisulu, Chief Luthuli, Yusuf Dadoo, Bram Fischer and Robert Sobukwe. Mandela: "depths of oppression" create "heights of character".
  • He says his country's greatest wealth is its people, finer and truer than the purest diamonds.
  • Courage is not the absence of fear but the triumph over it; the brave man conquers his fear.
  • Love and hate: no one is born hating another; people must learn to hate, and if they can learn to hate they can be taught to love. In prison he saw a glimmer of humanity in a guard. "Man's goodness is a flame that can be hidden but never extinguished."

💡 Courage is triumph over fear; hate is learned, love can be taught.

5️⃣ What freedom meant ⭐⭐⭐

  • Twin obligations: to one's family and to one's people, community and country. In South Africa a man of colour could hardly fulfil both and was forced into a twilight existence of secrecy and rebellion.
  • Boyhood freedom: he was born free (running in the fields, swimming in the stream), but the chapter says this was an illusion.
  • As a student he wanted freedom only for himself, the transitory freedoms of staying out at night, reading and going where he chose. In Johannesburg as a young man he wanted the basic and honourable freedoms: to achieve his potential, earn his keep, marry and have a family.
  • Then he saw that his brothers and sisters were not free either, and joined the ANC; his hunger for his own freedom became the hunger for the freedom of his people. The chapter says the oppressor and the oppressed alike are robbed of their humanity.

💡 Twin obligations; transitory and basic freedoms; both oppressor and oppressed are unfree.

6️⃣ Poem: A Tiger in the Zoo ⭐⭐⭐

  • Poet Leslie Norris. The poem contrasts a tiger in the zoo with a tiger in the jungle, moving from the zoo to the jungle and back to the zoo.
  • In the zoo the tiger stalks the few steps of his cage in his vivid stripes, on pads of velvet quiet, in his quiet rage.
  • In the wild he should be lurking in shadow, sliding through long grass near the water hole where plump deer pass, and terrorising the village.
  • At the end he is in a concrete cell, ignoring visitors, hearing the patrolling cars at night and staring with brilliant eyes at the brilliant stars. The chapter asks about the effect of the repeated words 'quiet' and 'brilliant'.

💡 Zoo versus jungle contrast; quiet rage; brilliant eyes and brilliant stars.

Q1 Oral Comprehension Check: where did the ceremonies take place
Answer dekho

In the sandstone amphitheatre formed by the Union Buildings in Pretoria. Naming sandstone public buildings in India is an open task and is not answered here.

Q2 Oral Comprehension Check: how is 10 May an autumn day in South Africa
Answer dekho

The chapter calls it a lovely autumn day. The text does not explain the season, so no reason is given here.

Q3 Oral Comprehension Check: what do the military generals do, and how has their attitude changed
Answer dekho

They saluted Mandela and pledged their loyalty. Not many years before, the chapter says, they would not have saluted but arrested him.

Q4 Oral Comprehension Check: why were two national anthems sung
Answer dekho

The day was symbolised for Mandela by whites singing Nkosi Sikelel iAfrika and blacks singing Die Stem, the old anthem of the Republic, which each group once despised. The chapter says they would soon know the words by heart.

Q5 Oral Comprehension Check: the systems of government in the first and final decades of the twentieth century
Answer dekho

In the first decade the white-skinned peoples erected a system of racial domination against the dark-skinned peoples, one of the harshest, most inhumane societies. In the last decade that system was overturned forever and replaced by one that recognised the rights and freedoms of all peoples, regardless of colour.

Q6 Oral Comprehension Check: what does courage mean to Mandela
Answer dekho

Courage is not the absence of fear but the triumph over it. The brave man is not he who does not feel afraid but he who conquers that fear.

Q7 Oral Comprehension Check: which is natural, to love or to hate
Answer dekho

Love. The chapter says people must learn to hate, and if they can learn to hate they can be taught to love, for love comes more naturally to the human heart than its opposite.

Q8 Oral Comprehension Check: the twin obligations
Answer dekho

One to his family (parents, wife and children) and one to his people, community and country.

Q9 Thinking about the Text 2: what does Mandela mean by the sum of all those African patriots
Answer dekho

He means that he stands for the many patriots before him whose suffering and courage made that day possible; the long and noble line ended and began again with him.

Q10 Thinking about the Text 4: how Mandela's understanding of freedom changed
Answer dekho

As a boy he felt free in a limited way. As a student he wanted freedom for himself, the transitory freedoms. As a young man in Johannesburg he wanted the basic and honourable freedoms. Then he saw that his people were not free and joined the ANC, and the hunger for his own freedom became the hunger for the freedom of his people.

Q11 Thinking about the Text 5: how his hunger for freedom changed his life
Answer dekho

The chapter says it transformed a frightened young man into a bold one, drove a law-abiding attorney to become a criminal, turned a family-loving husband into a man without a home and forced a life-loving man to live like a monk.

Q12 A Tiger in the Zoo: which stanzas are about the zoo and which about the jungle
Answer dekho

The first and fourth stanzas (and the last) describe the tiger in the zoo. The second and third stanzas describe the tiger in the jungle. Read from the poem's stanza order; the poem has five stanzas.
